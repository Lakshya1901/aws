Problem classification

What was wrong at the nearest mandi

Each load-day is classed by the nearest mandi's state that day (Section 9 Step 1 rules; below cost per D12). Delhi's arrival gluts were all banana at Azadpur; Kolar had no arrival glut this spring, only price falls.

Verification

Did the advised market really pay more?

Out-of-sample check on every load sent away from the nearest mandi: the advised and the nearest market's next real price report (within 7 days after the decision), minus freight. Above the zero line, the advice held. The diagonal is a perfect prediction.

Result · rescue

Unsold stock at Azadpur found a buyer every day

One 2-tonne tomato lot a day, harvested the day before. The engine proposed the spoiled share from that day's temperature and sent the edible part to the best-paying mandi within 100 km (D36).

Verification of the build

Checks behind these numbers

  • ✓Same code as production. The replay calls the advisor's own /plan and /recommend functions; only the date and the data source change.
  • ✓0 failed requests in 1,472 (1,380 loads in 184 plans, 92 unsold lots).
  • ✓Deterministic. Re-running 10-11 April gave identical output for all 30 loads.
  • ✓120 backend tests pass, including the Section 17.3 cases (normal week, glut day, ten-load split, rescue, recover order).
  • ✓Out of sample:
  • !Delhi cauliflower: the advice held on only 14 of 35 checkable loads (median realised gap Rs -0.49/kg). Cauliflower prices at the advised markets moved more than their history suggested.
  • !Kolar sent every load away because Bengaluru paid Rs 7-9/kg more after freight all spring; the price edge was real (903 of 903 held), but more trucks to one city would test the anti-herding cap, which Live mode cannot check without arrivals (D34).

All money and waste figures are estimates (D32). Loads and lots are simulated; prices, arrivals, weather and routes are real. Reproduce: python analysis/simulate_2026.py then python analysis/sim_summary.py; outputs in analysis/out/sim_2026*.json.
